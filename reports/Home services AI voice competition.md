# Win the contractors everyone else ignores

By November 2026 the contractor phone line has become one of the most contested spots in vertical AI. The well-funded specialists (Avoca at a **$1B valuation**, Netic at $450M, Probook with $40M from a16z and Sequoia, Broccoli with $52M from Khosla) are fighting over large PE-backed HVAC and plumbing platforms that run on ServiceTitan. The field-service platforms (ServiceTitan, Jobber, Housecall Pro, Workiz, FieldPulse) now sell their own AI receptionists, and Jobber's costs **$29/month**. Horizontal AI receptionists and agency resellers have pushed basic answering down to $25–$300/month, and human answering services cost 5–10x more and are losing on price. That leaves basic after-hours call capture commoditized at the bottom of the market and the enterprise tier taken at the top. The opening for a new entrant sits between them: owner-led contractors with about $2M–$20M in revenue, often with several locations. Most run FieldEdge, Successware or Service Fusion, which have no native AI agent, or are small roll-ups running several FSMs at once. These buyers need an agent that books revenue during the busy daytime hours, not one that only catches overflow and after-hours calls. The recommended wedge is a "revenue CSR" for that segment. It should write directly into the FSM, warm-transfer reliably, sell memberships and follow up on estimates, and be priced per booked job so the bill tracks the contractor's results, not the number of minutes. Two market events give it a selling hook. Google began billing Local Services Ads for missed calls in October 2026, and ServiceTitan has started restricting third-party AI vendors. One caveat applies throughout: nearly all pricing and complaint evidence comes from vendor and competitor blogs, and independent reviews barely exist.

## Capital has piled into the enterprise ServiceTitan tier

The most heavily funded companies all sell top-down to large operators. **Avoca** has raised more than $125M across seed through Series B. Its April 2026 round was co-led by Meritech and General Catalyst at a $1B valuation ([Avoca](https://avoca.ai/blog/avoca-raises-125m-series-b-1b-valuation); [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/04/27/avoca-ai-agents-missed-calls-hvac-plumbing-roofing-kleiner-perkins-chen-shrivastava-braswell/)). It reported **"eight figures" of ARR in 2025** and says it is on track to book $1B of jobs in 2026 ([Dealroom](https://dealroom.co/news/127930-avoca-raises-125m-at-1b-valuation-to-bring-ai-to-service-businesses/)). Named customers include TurnPoint, 1-800-GOT-JUNK? and Goettl, and Avoca's site lists Apex Service Partners and quotes a Sila-wide rollout ([Avoca](https://www.avoca.ai/)). It also has partnerships with ServiceTitan and Nexstar. Avoca does not publish prices. Third parties estimate **$1,000–$3,500/month**, billed largely per minute, with onboarding of **4–12 weeks** and no free trial ([FieldCamp](https://fieldcamp.ai/reviews/avoca-ai/); [Macha](https://www.getmacha.com/blog/avoca-ai-complete-guide)). Competitor reviewers call it over-engineered for shops under about $3M in revenue or with fewer than three CSRs ([gomecha](https://www.gomecha.ai/compare/avoca-ai)).

**Netic** has raised about $43M, including a $23M Series B led by Founders Fund at a $450M valuation ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-11-13/founders-fund-backs-startup-bringing-ai-to-plumbers-and-roofers)). It targets companies with **$20M to billions in revenue** and pitches revenue growth rather than cost cutting ([Inc.](https://www.inc.com/chloe-aiello/this-startup-gives-ai-superpowers-to-hvac-plumbing-and-roofing-companies/91266172)). Its founder says Netic has generated more than $600M for customers, a claim made without documentation ([No Priors](https://www.teahose.com/podcast/No%20Priors/Building%20an%20Autonomous%20Enterprise%20for%20Real-World%20Services%20with%20Netic%20Founder%20Melisa%20Tokmak)). **Probook** started from AI dispatch and has expanded into intake and outbound. Every customer it names is a large PE platform: TurnPoint, Master Trades Group, Del-Air, Peterman Brothers and Sila ([GlobeNewswire](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/06/23/3316215/0/en/probook-raises-40m-from-andreessen-horowitz-and-sequoia-to-scale-the-ai-operating-system-for-home-services.html)). **Lace AI** won Wrench Group in March 2026. Wrench's COO described the deal as "human plus AI" rather than CSR replacement ([Wrench Group](https://www.wrenchgroup.com/articles/wrench-group-announces-partnership-with-lace-ai)). TurnPoint appears on both Avoca's and Probook's customer lists, which suggests the largest platforms run several AI vendors side by side. A new entrant would arrive late to Apex, Wrench, Sila and TurnPoint.

Two players sit between the enterprise tier and small shops. **Broccoli** has raised $52M from Khosla at a reported valuation of about $300M ([FourWeekMBA](https://fourweekmba.com/ai-broccoli-ai-raises-52m-for-agents-that-book-plumbing-jobs/)). It claims **1,500+ customers, 10M+ conversations and 2.5M booked appointments**, and its product covers inbound answering, estimate follow-up, membership renewals and reactivation ([Founded CEO](https://foundedceo.substack.com/p/this-yc-backed-startup-raised-52)). **Hatch** sold to Yelp for **$270M in cash** plus $30M in retention. At the time it had about **$25M ARR growing ~70%**, so the price was roughly 11x ARR ([Yelp 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1345016/000134501626000011/yelpq42025ex992lettertos.htm)). Hatch sells annual per-location contracts that third parties estimate at $600–$1,500/month per location ([NextPhone](https://www.getnextphone.com/blog/hatch-ai-review)). The small-shop specialists are thinly funded. **Sameday** is one of the few that publishes prices: $449/month for 500 minutes and $789/month for 1,000 minutes, with no published overage rate. It integrates with ServiceTitan, Housecall Pro and Jobber ([golmtech](https://golmtech.solutions/blog/sameday-ai-answering-service-review/)). **LeadTruffle** is text-first and costs about $229–$629/month plus a $299 onboarding fee ([Stork](https://www.stork.ai/en/leadtruffle-2)). A stream of YC entrants (Rezora, Acrely, Bernard) shows how low the barrier to building a demo has fallen ([YC](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/industry/Home%20Services)). Rilla and Siro are funded heavily, but they coach in-home sales visits rather than answering phones.

## FSM-native agents commoditize after-hours answering but stop at overflow

Between August 2025 and April 2026, the three largest SMB and mid-market FSMs all shipped native AI phone agents ([astucia](https://astucia.io/blog/best-ai-receptionist-breezy-shutdown)). **Jobber's AI Receptionist** is the price anchor. It costs $29/month for 30 conversations, then $0.79 per extra conversation, and is included in the Plus plan ([StackSpace](https://stackspacesolutions.com/blog/jobber-ai-receptionist-review)). That puts a capable receptionist within reach of more than 100,000 Jobber customers ([Jobber media](https://www.getjobber.com/about/media/)). **Housecall Pro's CSR AI** is a quote-only add-on. As of February 2026, its "coming soon" list still included booking fallback and call-handling improvements ([HCP Feb 2026](https://www.housecallpro.com/resources/february-2026-product-updates/)). **Workiz's "Jessica"** requires customers to port their numbers to Workiz's phone plan, and when the monthly cap is exceeded it **stops answering and drops calls to voicemail** ([Workiz FAQ](https://help.workiz.com/hc/en-us/articles/30176041143697-Genius-Answering-FAQ)). FieldPulse shipped Operator AI in July 2025 ([FieldPulse](https://www.fieldpulse.com/resources/blog/2025-feature-roundup)), and Simpro has a native receptionist called Pulse.

ServiceTitan is the most dangerous incumbent. Its AI Voice Agent books directly against business units, job types and technician availability, and confirms and reschedules appointments ([ServiceTitan docs](https://help.servicetitan.com/docs/voice-agent-onboarding-part-1-set-up)). Third parties report a price of about **$2.75 per call**, but this is unconfirmed ([Roonly](https://roonly.ai/pricing/servicetitan-ai-voice-agent-pricing)). In Q2 FY27, virtual-agent revenue and call volume each **more than doubled quarter over quarter**. Management still calls adoption "early" and **concentrated in overflow and after-hours** ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/servicetitan-inc-q2-2027-earnings-123000022.html)). ServiceTitan has about 10,800 active customers and $82.1B in GTV ([10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1638826/000163882626000028/ttan-20260131.htm)). It is also building a perimeter around that base. Its API terms treat any use of an undisclosed AI system as a material breach and bar AI from autonomously choosing actions ([ServiceTitan API terms](https://www.servicetitan.com/legal/api-terms)). A June 2026 certification program adds revenue share ([FieldCamp](https://fieldcamp.ai/blog/servicetitan-integrations/)). In September 2026 ServiceTitan **cut off Podium's integration for about 1,000 shared customers** after Podium built competing AI agents ([SaaStr](https://www.saastr.com/servicetitan-just-shut-off-podiums-integration-for-1000-shared-customers-agents-turned-a-9-year-partner-into-a-direct-competitor/)). A startup whose core value depends on writing into ServiceTitan now carries a risk of being de-platformed.

The lane looks different below ServiceTitan. **FieldEdge has no first-party agent** and relies on Free2Grow ([TradeVulcan](https://tradevulcan.com/integrations/fieldedge)). **Service Fusion uses ZyraTalk** as a partner ([Service Fusion](https://www.servicefusion.com/blog/zyratalk-service-fusion-integration)). **Successware offers AI answering "only via third-party integrations"** ([TradesIndex](https://tradesindex.org/software/successware/)). ServiceTrade and BuildOps also have no native voice agent. Complaints about native agents follow a consistent pattern. They handle technical and pricing questions poorly, and their human handoff is weak; one second-hand r/HVAC anecdote describes a $15k system replacement lost because Jobber's bot could not transfer the caller ([Kore Komfort](https://korekomfortsolutions.com/jobber-ai-vs-housecall-pro-ai-are-they-worth-the-price-tag/)). The anecdote is unverified, but it matches a structural fact: native agents are built to catch overflow, not to close sales.

## Horizontal tools, answering services and agencies compete mainly on price

Horizontal AI receptionists anchor buyers' price expectations very low. **Rosie** starts at $49/month for 250 minutes. **Goodcall** charges $79/month for 100 unique callers. **Dialzara** starts at $29/month and is reportedly self-funded. **Smith.ai** runs an AI-first model with human backup at $150–$500/month for AI tiers and lists a ServiceTitan integration ([Techloy](https://www.techloy.com/8-best-ai-receptionists-for-home-services-in-2026/); [ContractorToolStack](https://contractortoolstack.com/software/dialzara/)). One 2026 roundup of 14 contractor-facing tools found prices starting at **$24.95/month** ([toolbelt](https://toolbelt-ai-contractors.netlify.app/ai-receptionists/)). Most of these tools integrate at the calendar or Zapier level. One review sums up the result: "if bookings queue for manual review, the system has not removed the work, it has moved it" ([thesnowmedia](https://thesnowmedia.com/resources/ai-receptionist-plumbing-hvac-options-compared/)). The fragility of the category shows in **Breezy AI**, a contractor after-hours receptionist that shut down in July 2026 ([astucia](https://astucia.io/blog/best-ai-receptionist-breezy-shutdown)).

The bigger horizontal threat comes from platforms with existing distribution. **Podium** launched an "AI Operating System for Home Services" in August 2026, with an AI employee called "Larry" that books onto the dispatch board ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/podium-launches-industrys-first-ai-170300892.html)). Its AI agent count reportedly doubled to more than 20,000 during 2026 ([Origin Notes](https://originnotes.substack.com/p/podium-gtm-playbook)). **Yelp** pairs the Hatch acquisition with a voice receptionist priced at $249+/month and a $30/month lead-reply agent ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/yelp-and-hatch-put-openais-gpt-live-1-into-voice-ai-after-1-million-calls/)). **RingCentral AIR** costs $39–$49/month, and **Quo's Sona** adds AI calls to a $19/user phone plan ([Voxipo](https://voxipo.com/pricing-guides/quo-sona/)).

Human answering services are pricing themselves out of the market. **Ruby** costs $250 for 50 minutes and up to $1,725 for 500, with overage of $3.30–$5.40/min ([SalesCaptain](https://blog.salescaptain.com/ruby-receptionists-pricing-2026-is-it-worth-it/)). **AnswerConnect** costs $350 for 200 minutes ([Oncrew](https://oncrew.ai/blog/best-answering-service-hvac-companies-2026)). Trade call centers charge **$1.50–$7 per answered call** or $25–$38 per hour for onshore agents ([CallForce](https://callforce.global/services/answering-service/hvac-cost/)). Some are adding AI overflow billed per minute on top of dedicated CSRs at $52.50/hour ([ContactCenterUSA](https://contactcenterusa.com/blog/after-hours-answering-service-hvac-companies)). Ruby publicly refuses to replace people with AI ([CloudTalk](https://www.cloudtalk.io/blog/ruby-receptionist-review/)). The agency channel undercuts everyone. GoHighLevel's unbundled Voice AI costs about **$0.07–$0.25/min all-in** ([eSEOspace](https://eseospace.com/blog/gohighlevel-voice-ai-cost/)), and agencies resell it inside marketing retainers at about $297/month ([getlocalcall](https://getlocalcall.com/best-ai-answering-service-for-contractors)). A contractor using 300 minutes a month costs an agency only about $20–$75 to serve. That economics explains the flood of generic, write-back-free bots, and it means **raw minutes are not a moat for anyone**.

Complaints across these three groups center on billing and on configuration rather than voice quality. Smith.ai customers report auto-transfers to paid live agents that inflated bills ([ContractorToolStack](https://contractortoolstack.com/software/smith-ai/)). Buyers are warned to ask what happens to the bill "in a busy week" ([Techloy](https://www.techloy.com/8-best-ai-receptionists-for-home-services-in-2026/)). Deployments fail through wrong calendars, vague service areas and missing emergency paths ([Lunacal](https://lunacal.ai/blogs/ai-receptionist-for-contractors)). Per-minute pricing also has a built-in flaw: **bills peak in the busiest seasonal months** ([getnextphone](https://www.getnextphone.com/blog/ai-receptionist-pricing)).

## The open gap is revenue-producing calls at mid-size shops off ServiceTitan

The demand side is large and still poorly served. There are about **111,000 plumbing and HVAC employer establishments** and 83,000 electrical ones, and **76.8% have fewer than 10 employees** ([Technology.org](https://www.technology.org/2026/11/05/how-many-plumbers-and-electricians-are-in-the-us-4-counts/)). Invoca's 2026 benchmarks found that a person answered only **52% of inbound home-services calls** ([Invoca](https://invoca.com/reports/the-invoca-home-services-lead-conversion-benchmarks-report-2026)). Average HVAC repair revenue reached **$1,205 per job in 2025** ([Housecall Pro](https://www.housecallpro.com/resources/hvac-industry-trends/)). Since October 1, 2026, Google LSA has billed unanswered business-hours calls that ring more than 20 seconds as leads ([Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/google-local-services-ads-will-charge-for-some-missed-calls-starting-oct-1-485798)). Missing calls now costs contractors directly, on top of the lost job. Adoption still trails intent: 74% of contractors see AI as essential, but only about 25% use it, and they cite complexity and "not knowing where to start" as the barriers, not price ([ServiceTitan report](https://www.servicetitan.com/press/servicetitan-report-finds-74-of-residential-contractors-see-ai-as-key)).

Putting the sections together shows where the space is open. Small Jobber and Housecall Pro shops already have a $29–$200 native option for basic answering. ServiceTitan enterprises are claimed by Avoca, Netic, Probook and Lace, and by ServiceTitan itself. The least-served segment is **independents and small multi-location operators with roughly $2M–$20M in revenue**. They are too small for Avoca's 4–12 week enterprise rollout and Netic's $20M floor, but their call volume and job values make a $29 overflow bot a poor fit. Many run **FieldEdge, Successware or Service Fusion, which have no native agent** and only a few partner vendors. PE roll-ups add a second structural gap. They inherit a mix of FSMs from acquired shops and migrate them to ServiceTitan over time ([CT Acquisitions](https://ctacquisitions.com/how-pe-roll-ups-unlock-value-in-home-services/)), so one native agent cannot cover every location mid-migration. LEK counts **at least 500 PE-backed services platforms** ([LEK](https://www.lek.com/sites/default/files/2026-09/positioning-scaled-services-platforms-for-next-level-growth-and-exit-ei.pdf)). Most sit well below Apex and Wrench, and new platforms such as American Service Partners keep forming ([citybiz](https://www.citybiz.co/?p=915499)).

The capability gap points the same way. Native agents and horizontal tools are built to capture overflow, but the money in a contractor's call mix comes from booking at peak hours, replacement and sales calls, and memberships. Membership handling appears in vendor copy only as renewal reminders, not as selling during the call ([Harmony](https://harmony.ai/use-case/home-services)). Consultants tell PE platforms to turn the call center into a sales center ([Alvarez & Marsal](https://www.alvarezandmarsal.com/sites/default/files/2025-03/9549_A%26M_460483_PEPI_Trading%20Up%20Industrials%20Article_PK_Stg04.pdf)). Consumer resistance is real but can be overcome. A survey by a live-answering vendor found **87% prefer a human** for local home services, yet **73% say resolution matters more than answer speed** ([PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/serviceforge-releases-new-report-revealing-consumer-pushback-against-ai-driven-customer-service-302672345.html)). In Invoca's 2026 survey, the share of home-services buyers saying AI made their experience worse fell from 20% to 16% ([Invoca](https://www.invoca.com/reports/home-services-buyer-experience-report-2026)). An agent that books, quotes and hands off cleanly to a person answers the objection that customers hate robots. One that only takes messages does not. Some sub-trades also look underserved, though this is inferred from silence in the sources rather than proven. Avoca is expanding into moving, junk removal and auto rather than restoration, pest, pool or septic ([Pulse 2.0](https://pulse2.com/avoca-125-million-raised-at-1-billion-valuation-to-power-services-economy-with-ai/)), and none of the specialists names pest-control customers.

## A concrete wedge for a small founding team

Seven differentiation strategies follow from these gaps. Each targets a weakness documented above:

1. **Resolution, not answering.** Write jobs, customers and leads directly into the FSM, so no booking queues for manual re-entry.
2. **Primary-line coverage at peak hours, with guaranteed warm transfer.** Use a visible human handoff, rules for "never let a $15k replacement call die in the bot," and live routing to on-call techs for gas, no-heat and flooding calls.
3. **Revenue skills that native agents lack.** These include membership offers on every eligible call, price-range answers tied to the shop's price book, financing pre-qualification, and outbound follow-up on unsold estimates and lapsed members.
4. **Carrier-agnostic deployment.** Forward calls from the existing RingCentral, call-tracking or carrier line instead of requiring a port, as Workiz does.
5. **Spanish-first flows.** Treat Spanish as a native flow rather than a translated script.
6. **Proof of value every month.** Report booked jobs and revenue attributed to the agent, plus LSA missed-call charges avoided, since vendors identify "forgetting the AI is working" as a leading churn reason ([myvoiceaiconnect](https://www.myvoiceaiconnect.com/blog/sell-ai-receptionist-to-home-service-contractors)).
7. **Self-serve setup in days, not weeks.** Underwrite it with trade templates, so the "operational complexity" barrier disappears.

The recommended wedge combines these strategies into a single offer:

| Dimension | Recommendation | Rationale from the evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Target segment | Residential HVAC and plumbing (then electrical) with $2M–$20M revenue and 1–10 locations on **FieldEdge, Successware or Service Fusion**. Expand second into small PE platforms running mixed FSMs. | No native agent on these FSMs; too small for Avoca and Netic; too valuable for $29 bots; avoids ServiceTitan's API and revenue-share risk |
| Product scope | A "revenue CSR" that answers the primary line during peak hours, overflow and after-hours calls. It books into the FSM, warm-transfers, triages emergencies, offers memberships, quotes ranges, and runs outbound estimate and membership follow-up. Add ServiceTitan only as a certified partner later. | Native agents are confined to overflow; Broccoli and Avoca show outbound and memberships drive value |
| Pricing | A low platform fee (about $199–$299/mo) plus **a fee per FSM-verified booked job** (roughly $10–$25, benchmarked against call centers' $8–$25 per confirmed appointment) with a monthly cap and storm-season ceiling. Free after-hours pilot. | Per-minute pricing punishes peak months; outcome billing is rare in this market; wholesale voice costs of $0.07–$0.25/min leave ample margin |
| Go-to-market | Founder-led sales opened with the LSA missed-call billing change. Partner listings on FieldEdge, Successware and Service Fusion. Contractor coaching groups and HVAC distributors. Revenue-share partnerships with the marketing agencies that already own these contractors' LSA accounts. | Contractors cite complexity, not price; agencies already control the relationship; open-lane FSMs need voice partners |

The per-booked-job benchmark comes from outsourced trade call centers ([ContactCenterUSA](https://contactcenterusa.com/blog/top-10-hvac-home-services-call-center-companies-usa)). Wholesale voice-minute costs are documented by GoHighLevel pricing guides ([eSEOspace](https://eseospace.com/blog/gohighlevel-voice-ai-cost/)). Pricing per booked job also enforces product discipline. It only works if the FSM integration can verify each booking, so the integration becomes the moat rather than a checkbox. The pitch becomes "you pay when we put a job on the board," a direct answer to the opaque per-minute bills buyers complain about. **Restoration** (water, fire and mold) is worth testing as a parallel wedge. It is 24/7, emergency-driven and high-ticket, and it adds insurance-claim intake complexity that HVAC-tuned agents do not handle; no vendor in the research targets it. That hypothesis rests on an absence of evidence and should be validated with operator interviews before committing to it.

The risks are concrete. Jobber, Housecall Pro and ServiceTitan will keep moving upmarket from overflow into primary answering. FieldEdge or Successware could ship a native agent or sign an exclusive partner. Analysts already flag foundation-model commoditization of voice agents by 2027 ([Enterprise DNA](https://enterprisedna.co/resources/news/avoca-voice-ai-trades-unicorn-2026)). A small team's durable assets are therefore write-back integrations into under-served FSMs, a library of trade-specific call outcomes, and attribution data that proves revenue impact. Voice quality, which any GoHighLevel agency can now buy wholesale, is not a durable asset. The evidence base has clear limits as well. No published prices exist for Avoca, Netic, Probook, Broccoli or Hatch. Contractor complaints came almost entirely from competitor-authored blogs, and first-hand Reddit and review data could not be retrieved. Customer counts for the FieldEdge, Successware and Service Fusion bases were not found, so the target segment's size must be confirmed directly.

## Conclusion

This market has inverted the usual startup playbook. The obvious entry points are a cheap SMB receptionist or a deep ServiceTitan integration, and both are now the most defended positions. FSMs give answering away at $29, and ServiceTitan showed with Podium that it will cut off AI partners that compete with its own agent. Value is shifting from answering a call to proving that a call became revenue. The Hatch exit at about 11x ARR and Avoca's pitch of "$1B of booked jobs" show that acquirers and investors are paying for booked outcomes, not minutes handled.

For a small team, the winning move is to own the revenue CSR role in the segment the giants are structurally unable or unwilling to serve: mid-size, owner-led and multi-FSM contractors off ServiceTitan. Charging per verified booking turns the integration into the moat, the monthly report into the retention engine, and the Google LSA billing change into the opening line of every sales call. The first 90 days should test two questions before anything else is built. First, can the team reach write-back depth on FieldEdge, Successware or Service Fusion quickly? Second, will owners in that band accept per-booked-job pricing?
