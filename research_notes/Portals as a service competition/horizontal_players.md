Direct page fetches to techcrunch.com, dealroom.co and michaellivs.com failed in this sandbox because those hosts were not allowed. ## Players: positioning, verticals, pricing, funding, traction, customers

### Takeaway
The field splits into three layers. (1) Raw cloud-browser infrastructure (Browserbase, Kernel, Steel, Hyperbrowser, Anchor, Browser Use Cloud) has converged on about $0.05–0.12 per browser-hour pricing. (2) Agent frameworks and builders (Skyvern, Stagehand, Browser Use OSS, Integuru) are largely open source. (3) A few "managed API for any website" companies sell user-permissioned, logged-in access billed per successful call (Deck; Anon until Nov 2026). The most important recent event is **Sierra acquiring Anon on Nov 5, 2026**, which removes the best-known horizontal "user-agent API" player from the independent market and folds it into a vertical/CX agent platform.

### Inferences
- The infra layer is effectively commoditized. Five or more vendors sell near-identical cloud Chromium at $0.05–0.12/hr, and the open-source agent layer (Browser Use MIT, Skyvern AGPL, Stagehand, Integuru) is free. Frontier models expose computer use natively at about 72–75% OSWorld-Verified. The core "agent drives a website" capability is therefore not a moat in Nov 2026.
- Defensibility sits above the infra: per-portal maintained workflows, credential/MFA vaulting, success-based SLAs, auditability and vertical data normalization. The per-successful-call model (Deck) and Anon's acquisition by an application company (Sierra) suggest that horizontal "API for any website" businesses either become vertical-specific or get absorbed.
- Anon's exit (Nov 2026) reduces horizontal competition for a carrier-portal API startup, but signals that CX/agent platforms (Sierra, and plausibly peers) will build behind-login capability in-house.
- The only incumbent-like vertical competitor surfaced for insurance is CoverForce, which leans on real carrier APIs plus agents rather than pure portal driving.

## Reliability, auth/MFA, CAPTCHA, ToS/legal, and complaints

### Takeaway
Anti-bot friction got worse in 2026. Cloudflare's default agent-blocking presets took effect Sep 15, 2026. US legal risk under the CFAA eased after the Ninth Circuit's Aug 4, 2026 Amazon v. Perplexity decision, which treats a user-directed agent as the user. That holding is narrow and leaves ToS/tort claims open. Logged-in, user-permissioned access (the carrier-portal model) is the most legally defensible pattern.

### Cited Findings
- **Cloudflare:** splits bots into Search/Agent/Training categories. Under defaults effective **Sep 15, 2026**, ad-supported sites block "Agent" on pages with ads — not a web-wide block — [o-mega](https://o-mega.ai/articles/cloudflare-blocks-ai-agents-by-default-2026); [anakin.io](https://anakin.io/blog/cloudflare-ai-agent-blocking-default-2026); [Medium](https://medium.com/@decoding_ai_by_nureravi/websites-will-block-ai-agents-by-default-on-september-15-is-your-agent-ready-174b6901fd5c)
- Agents trigger CAPTCHAs and 403/429 errors because of datacenter IPs, automated-browser fingerprints and missing logins. In a 2026 benchmark across 31 Cloudflare-protected sites, results varied by tool — [Reduck blog](https://blog.reduck.ai/bypass-cloudflare-and-datadome-with-your-own-chrome/). Blocked agents often receive managed challenges that headless stacks cannot clear, rather than a clean 403 — [nerdleveltech](https://nerdleveltech.com/cloudflare-blocks-ai-agents-fallback-pattern)
- **Amazon v. Perplexity (Comet):** N.D. Cal. granted a preliminary injunction (Mar 9–10, 2026) — [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/03/10/amazon-wins-court-order-to-block-perplexitys-ai-shopping-agent.html). The Ninth Circuit vacated it on **Aug 4, 2026**, holding that the user, not the agent developer, "accesses" the computers under the CFAA/CDAFA. The court expressly declined to create "a new legal regime governing agentic AI" and did not address tort or ToS claims — [ABA](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/litigation/resources/newsletters/privacy-data-security/ninth-circuit-holds-human-user-not-developer-ai-agent-responsible-websites-accessed/); [WSGR](https://www.wsgr.com/en/insights/ninth-circuit-addresses-cfaa-and-agentic-ai-tools-in-groundbreaking-decision.html); [MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/417007/appeals-court-sides-against-amazon-lifts-perplexi.html). Rehearing was denied Sep 10, 2026; Amazon filed an amended complaint on Sep 21, 2026, and the case was still active in early Nov 2026 — [nohacks](https://nohacks.co/blog/amazon-perplexity-cfaa-agent-visitor-rights)
- Credential and auth handling is a core product: Anon's stack centered on auth and credential handling — [USV](https://www.usv.com/writing/2026/05/our-investment-in-anon-2/). Deck positions itself on "user-permissioned" access — [PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/news/investment-tracker/2025/deck-raises-12-million-dollars-infrastructure-accesses-user-permissioned-data/)
- Browser Use migrated from Playwright to direct CDP in early 2026, an industry move toward lower-level control for reliability and stealth — [Anchor blog](https://anchorbrowser.io/blog/best-browser-infrastructure-computer-use-agents-2026)

### Inferences
- Carrier portals are authenticated B2B sites, often without consumer-style Cloudflare agent presets, so CAPTCHA risk is probably lower than on consumer sites. MFA/OTP handling and session persistence will dominate reliability.
- The Ninth Circuit's reasoning ("agent = user") is favorable when an agency or customer delegates its own carrier credentials. Contract (ToS/agency-agreement) risk with carriers remains and was not addressed.

### Gaps
- No primary HN/Reddit/G2 complaint threads were retrieved; search returned vendor blogs only. Flake rates and MFA failure rates per vendor were not found.
- No carrier-specific ToS enforcement cases were found.

## Legacy RPA incumbents' agentic offerings

### Takeaway
UiPath, SS&C Blue Prism and Automation Anywhere have all rebranded around "agentic automation" and offer governed, on-prem-capable agent stacks. They compete through existing enterprise (carrier/insurer) relationships rather than as per-transaction API vendors.

### Cited Findings
- **UiPath:** the Agent Builder, Maestro orchestration, ScreenPlay (LLM-driven UI agent), Healing Agent (self-repairing selectors) and context grounding were brought on-prem in the Automation Suite release (Apr 2026), including for the public sector — [UiPath IR](https://ir.uipath.com/news/detail/446/uipath-automation-suite-delivers-on-premises-agentic-ai-for-the-public-sector); [UiPath blog](https://www.uipath.com/blog/product-and-updates/agentic-ai-now-where-your-data-lives). Studio Web is positioned for browser-based agentic/API-first automation. The Autopilot Agent coding agent entered preview on Sep 25, 2026 — [UiPath release notes Sep 2026](https://docs.uipath.com/agents/automation-cloud/latest/release-notes/september-2026)
- **SS&C Blue Prism:** launched WorkHQ (Apr 2026), an agentic platform coordinating agents, robots and people. It also offers the SS&C AI Gateway for governance and audit logs, and an "Agentic Workflow" offering on its 2026 roadmap (vendor material) — [Blue Prism roadmap](https://www.blueprism.com/resources/blog/agentic-automation-roadmap-2026/). A third-party site says Blue Prism slipped from Leader to Challenger/Niche in analyst rankings since the SS&C acquisition (unverified) — [automationatlas](https://automationatlas.io/guides/automation-anywhere-vs-blue-prism-rpa-comparison/)
- **Automation Anywhere:** positioned as cloud-native "agentic process automation." Ranked #2 in RPA by PeerSpot user rating versus Blue Prism #5 — [PeerSpot](https://www.peerspot.com/products/comparisons/automation-anywhere_vs_blue-prism)
- **Uniphore** (enterprise AI) bought Orby AI (Aug 2025) to add agentic workflow automation, showing consolidation of computer-use startups into larger platforms — [Seedtable](https://seedtable.com/exits/orby-ai)

### Inferences
- Incumbents sell licenses and platforms to the enterprise that owns the workflow, such as a carrier or large broker. They do not sell a hosted per-transaction API to third parties. A Portals-as-a-Service startup competes with them mainly when a large customer prefers to build in-house with existing RPA, and "self-healing" UI agents (UiPath Healing Agent, ScreenPlay) narrow the maintenance-cost argument against RPA.

### Gaps
- No Automation Anywhere 2026 product specifics or pricing were retrieved. No incumbent insurance-portal case studies were found.
