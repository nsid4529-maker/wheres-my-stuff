# US Home Services Contractor Buyers, Market Structure, and White Space for AI Voice Agents (as of Nov 2026)

Method note: research was done on 2026-11-23. Direct page fetches were blocked in this session (servicetitan.com, housecallpro.com and invoca.com would not resolve), so **every finding below comes from search-result snippets and summaries, not from the full primary documents**. Treat exact percentages as needing verification against the originals. Many sources are vendor marketing; these are flagged. Dates are given for each fact. Anything dated before 2025 is flagged [POSSIBLY STALE].

## 1. Market structure: business counts, size, job values, call volumes, missed calls

### Takeaway
The core "phone-driven" trades number in the hundreds of thousands of US establishments, and they are overwhelmingly small: about 77% of plumbing/HVAC establishments have fewer than 10 employees. Missed-call benchmarks run from 14% (CallRail) to about 27% (Invoca), and newer Invoca data says a human answers only about 52% of inbound calls. With repair tickets of about $400 to $1,200 or more, each missed call is worth hundreds of dollars. Dollar figures for annual losses come from vendors and lack a clear method.

### Cited Findings
**Business counts (Census County Business Patterns, reference year 2023, released 2025, mostly via secondary blogs):**
- Plumbing, heating and A/C contractors (NAICS 238220): about 111,207 establishments with paid employees, about 1.21M employees. 76.8% have fewer than 10 employees. Census does not split HVAC from plumbing. — [Technology.org, Nov 2026](https://www.technology.org/2026/11/05/how-many-plumbers-and-electricians-are-in-the-us-4-counts/); [localprospects.ai](https://localprospects.ai/blog/how-many-hvac-companies-in-the-us); [hyrehvac](https://hyrehvac.com/statistics/hvac-industry/)
- Electrical contractors (NAICS 23821): 83,342 employer establishments (2023), plus about 171,644 nonemployer businesses (2024). — [Technology.org, Nov 2026](https://www.technology.org/2026/11/05/how-many-plumbers-and-electricians-are-in-the-us-4-counts/)
- Roofing (NAICS 23816): estimates vary from about 25,519 (Census, cited by a blog) to 31,667 (Home Service Opportunity Index 2026) to about 35,000. — [Pipelineon 2026 Opportunity Index](https://pipelineon.com/blog/2026-home-service-opportunity-index/); [knockio](https://knockio.com/blog/what-is-a-home-service-business/)
- Pest control: 16,535 (Census, cited by a blog) vs. 34,076 (another source). The two counts conflict. — [boomcycle](https://boomcycle.com/blog/home-services-marketing-statistics/); [aiclientbuilder](https://aiclientbuilder.com/stats/home-services-industry-data)
- Landscaping (NAICS 561730): counts conflict at 117,969, 123,344 and 556,238. The largest figure likely includes nonemployers or a broader category. — [Monday Morning Memo](https://www.mondaymorningmemo.com/how-many-home-services-companies-are-there-in-america/); [knockio](https://knockio.com/blog/what-is-a-home-service-business/)
- No reliable counts were found for garage door, pool service, septic, appliance repair or restoration in this pass (see Gaps).

- Tampa Bay, 2026: median HVAC and plumbing invoice $493; median water-heater repair $1,510. - Plumbing national average ticket $315 ($856 for premium operators); electrician $348 to $419; roofing $7,500 to $12,000. All from a 2025 third-party compilation of low authority. — [homeservicehound, 2025](https://homeservicehound.pages.dev/blog/average-home-service-ticket-prices-2025)

**Missed calls and answer rates:**
- Invoca: 27% of calls to home services businesses are not answered. The original date is unclear and may predate 2025 [POSSIBLY STALE]. — [Invoca blog](https://invoca.com/blog/how-much-missed-sales-calls-cost-home-services-businesses)
- Invoca 2026 Home Services Lead Conversion Benchmarks (reported July 2026): a person answered 52% of all inbound calls and 65% of calls longer than 15 seconds. — [Invoca 2026 Home Services Benchmarks](https://invoca.com/reports/the-invoca-home-services-lead-conversion-benchmarks-report-2026)
- CallRail small-business benchmark (Jan 2025): home services missed-call rate 14%, vs. health care 32% and legal 28%. — cited via [skipcalls](https://skipcalls.com/blog/missed-call-benchmarks-2025-contractors); [CallRail blog](https://www.callrail.com/blog/save-home-services-business-from-missed-calls-lost-jobs)
- Outlier vendor data: 74.1% unanswered across 130,175 calls from 45 businesses. This sample is not representative. — [search summary of vendor research](https://stealthagents.com/research/home-services-missed-call-revenue-statistics-2026)
- An "85% of callers who reach voicemail call a competitor" figure circulates widely, and "$75K to $100K+ lost per year" estimates appear on vendor sites. Neither could be traced to a primary study. — [Signpost](https://www.signpost.com/blog/how-much-business-do-contractors-lose-from-missed-calls/); [getnextphone](https://www.getnextphone.com/blog/ai-receptionist-missed-calls-solution)
- Invoca Home Services Buyer Experience Report 2026 (134 US/UK consumers, May 8 to 22, 2026): two-thirds hang up after being on hold too long, and about one third would choose an AI assistant to avoid a hold queue. These figures come from a secondary summary. — [Invoca report](https://www.invoca.com/reports/home-services-buyer-experience-report-2026)
- Invoca cross-industry B2C report 2026: 79% of consumers would switch to a competitor that responds faster. — [Marketing Dive, Jun 8 2026](https://www.marketingdive.com/press-release/20260608-new-invoca-study-finds-consumers-wont-wait-ai-gives-brands-the-speed-to-w/)

### Inferences
- A rough calculation: if a 52% to 86% answer rate is applied to repair tickets of $400 to $1,200, a typical small HVAC or plumbing shop leaks thousands of dollars a month in calls. This supports outcome-linked ROI messaging. Inference only; no primary study confirms annual dollar loss.
- The long tail is huge: well over 75% of trade establishments have fewer than 10 employees. They lack a dedicated customer service rep (CSR), which is the core market for low-cost AI receptionists. The PE platforms (Section 2) are the high-ACV enterprise segment with very different needs.

### Gaps
- No verified establishment counts for garage door, pool service, septic, appliance repair or water and fire restoration. These trades sit inside broader NAICS codes (for example, 561790 or 811412), and pages could not be fetched.
- No reliable per-business inbound call-volume figure (calls per month by trade). ServiceTitan and Invoca reports likely contain one but could not be fetched.
- The original dates and methodology of the Invoca 27% figure could not be verified.

## 2. Private-equity roll-ups and franchise systems as a channel

### Takeaway
PE consolidation is intense. LEK estimates at least 500 PE-backed residential and commercial services platforms, and valuations are rising (Apex reportedly about $10B; Flint Group above $1.5B). Large platforms centralize call centers, and they are actively buying AI voice: Wrench chose Lace AI (March 2026), and Avoca claims Apex and Sila as customers. The positioning that sells to these buyers is "human plus AI" augmentation of the call center, not replacing CSRs. Franchisors (Neighborly with 19 brands) use AI but have not publicly named a voice-agent vendor.

### Cited Findings
- LEK (Sept 2026): at least 500 PE-backed residential and commercial services platforms in the US. — [LEK, 2026](https://www.lek.com/sites/default/files/2026-09/positioning-scaled-services-platforms-for-next-level-growth-and-exit-ei.pdf)
- Hedge Think (early 2026): more than 30 PE-backed platforms actively acquiring in North American home services. This conflicts in scope with LEK, which counts all services. — [Hedge Think](https://www.hedgethink.com/the-capital-stack-behind-home-services-pe-roll-ups/)
- Capstone Partners (Oct 2026): PE activity and valuations remain strong in residential services. Competition for add-ons has made new platform formation harder, pushing sponsors toward adjacent categories. — [Capstone Oct 2026 report](https://www.capstonepartners.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Capstone-Partners_Residential-Services_MA-Coverage-Report_October-2026.pdf); [Capstone insight, Oct 9 2026](https://capstonepartners.com/insights/report-residential-services-ma-update)
- HVAC services deal mix: 38 deals (41.3%) were PE add-ons, and new platform creation fell from 10 to 9. Apex reportedly valued at about $10B. — [TradeVulcan, 2026](https://tradevulcan.com/dispatch/home-services-ma-private-equity-add-ons-2026)
- Permira agreed to buy a majority stake in Flint Group (HVAC, plumbing and electrical roll-up), valuing it above $1.5B, expected to close by end of 2026. — [Finimize, citing Reuters](https://finimize.com/content/permira-is-buying-into-the-booming-us-home-services-roll-up)
- American Service Partners launched a national home services platform across 15 states (Oct 7, 2026). — [citybiz](https://www.citybiz.co/?p=915499); [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/small-business/articles/american-partners-launches-national-home-121700580.html)
- Apex Service Partners: more than 75 local brands, 46 states, more than 13,000 employees, centralized call-center operations, technology and analytics. Profile is from a firmographic aggregator. — [akta.pro](https://akta.pro/company-profiles/0004gqw/apex-service-partners)
- Wrench Group: 27 markets in 14 states. In March 2026 it partnered with **Lace AI** to use AI voice, text and email for inbound and outbound workflows, including scheduling, receptionist, speed-to-lead, after-hours coverage and follow-up, plus call-center coaching. The COO framed it as human plus AI rather than replacing staff. — [Wrench Group announcement](https://www.wrenchgroup.com/articles/wrench-group-announces-partnership-with-lace-ai); [Contractor Mag](https://www.contractormag.com/technology/news/55361726/wrench-group-teams-with-lace-ai-to-boost-call-center-performance)
- Avoca's site lists Apex Service Partners as a client and quotes Sila Services' CTO on a Sila-wide rollout. This is vendor marketing. — [Avoca](https://www.avoca.ai/)
- Sila Services' footprint is concentrated in the Northeast, Mid-Atlantic and Midwest. — [dealseam PE tracker 2026](https://dealseam.com/hvac-pe-rollup-tracker-2026)
- Consultants advise platforms to turn the call center into a sales center (A&M, Mar 2025). West Monroe says winners "get the first call and keep the customer for life," and that centralizing data for AI is a priority. — [Alvarez & Marsal, 2025](https://www.alvarezandmarsal.com/sites/default/files/2025-03/9549_A%26M_460483_PEPI_Trading%20Up%20Industrials%20Article_PK_Stg04.pdf); [West Monroe](https://westmonroe.com/insights/private-equity-investment-in-residential-services)
- Neighborly (19 brands) launched an AI-powered consumer app in June 2026 and uses AI in marketing. A secondary Vendasta case says about 30% of Neighborly leads had previously gone uncontacted, and that AI intake produced 35,700 conversations and 3,400 conversions. That claim needs verification. — [Franchising.com, Jun 15 2026](https://www.franchising.com/amp/news/20260615_neighborly_launches_redesigned_aipowered_app_to_simplify_home_service_manag.html); [Vendasta](https://www.vendasta.com/blog/after-hours-answering-service/)
- Franchising.com (Nov 10, 2026, sponsored) warns of "secret costs" of AI receptionists in franchise systems. Multiple vendors (Nevermiss, Rosie, Goodcall and others) target franchise systems with brand-voice white-label agents. — [Franchising.com](https://www.franchising.com/articles/20261110_the_secret_cost_of_ai_receptionists_in_franchise_systems.html); [ecommercefastlane](https://ecommercefastlane.com/ai-customer-service-trends-2026-franchise/)
- Avoca has partnerships with ServiceTitan, Nexstar and 1-800-GOT-JUNK (a franchise system). — [Pulse 2.0, Apr 2026](https://pulse2.com/avoca-125-million-raised-at-1-billion-valuation-to-power-services-economy-with-ai/)

### Inferences
- The top tier of PE platforms is already being contested by well-funded specialists (Avoca and Lace). A new entrant is likely late to Apex, Wrench and Sila. The more open opportunity may be the long tail of roughly 500 smaller and mid-size platforms, and newly formed ones such as American Service Partners and Flint post-close. Roll-up integration creates a recurring need: each acquired brand must be onboarded onto a shared call flow and FSM.
- "Human plus AI" (coaching, overflow, after-hours, outbound) is the framing PE COOs use publicly. A new entrant pitching full CSR replacement to platforms may meet resistance.
- Franchisors are a channel with a distinct buying need: brand consistency across franchisees, roll-up reporting for the franchisor and local pricing. No major home-services franchisor (Neighborly, Authority Brands) has publicly named a voice-agent vendor, so this may be white space.

### Gaps
- Location counts and call-center tech stacks for Turnpoint, Leap Partners, Southern Home Services, Authority Brands and Sila did not appear in search results.
- No data on what share of PE platforms have deployed AI voice.
- Wrench ownership is conflicting across sources (Leonard Green vs. Apax). This was not resolved.

## 3. Contractor attitudes and consumer acceptance; what is missing

### Takeaway
Contractors are interested but slow to adopt. Reported AI use ranges from about 25% (ServiceTitan) to 48% (Housecall Pro), and front-office and response speed are the leading use cases. Consumer surveys from the live-answering lobby show strong preference for humans (about 87% to 90%). Invoca's smaller survey shows AI sentiment in home services improving. The trust problem is real but softening. Vendors claim strength in emergency triage and Spanish, but this is unverified, and membership selling is barely addressed.

### Cited Findings
- ServiceTitan 2026 Residential State of the Trades (about 1,000 contractors): 74% see AI as an efficiency engine, but only about 25% currently use AI. 73% say early adoption gives a competitive edge. The biggest barriers are operational complexity and not knowing where to start. Early impact is in office, scheduling, dispatch and admin. — [ServiceTitan press](https://www.servicetitan.com/press/servicetitan-report-finds-74-of-residential-contractors-see-ai-as-key); [ACHR News](https://www.achrnews.com/articles/166074-survey-25-of-residential-contractors-using-ai-to-boost-ai); [EC Mag](https://www.ecmag.com/magazine/articles/article-detail/a.i.-adoption-among-residential-contractors-lags-despite-perceived-benefits)
- Housecall Pro "The AI Advantage" (June 2026, 248 pros): 48% actively use AI. "Faster customer response and fewer lost leads" is the most-cited business change. — [HCP report PDF](https://www.housecallpro.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/062026-HCP-The-AI-Advantage-report.pdf)
- More than 70% of home service pros use AI for admin work but not field jobs. — [Contractor Mag](https://www.contractormag.com/technology/article/55294441/more-than-70-of-home-service-pros-use-ai-to-cut-admin-work-but-not-field-jobs)
- ServiceForge "Keep Service Human" (January 2026; OnePoll, 6,000 adults, cross-industry): nearly 9 in 10 prefer a human over an AI bot. 83% have actively requested a human. 54% find AI customer service frustrating. 73% say resolution matters more than answer speed. A June 2026 follow-up found 87% would rather speak with a real person when contacting a local home service. **ServiceForge sells live answering, so it has a conflict of interest.** — [PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/serviceforge-releases-new-report-revealing-consumer-pushback-against-ai-driven-customer-service-302672345.html); [Contracting Business](https://www.contractingbusiness.com/industry-news/news/55356376/serviceforge-survey-shows-hvac-customers-prefer-human-service-representatives); [PM Mag](https://www.pmmag.com/articles/107528-serviceforge-survey-highlights-demand-for-human-interaction)
- Counterpoint, Invoca 2026 (134 respondents): the share saying AI made their home services buying experience worse fell from 20% (2025) to 16% (2026). 54% of home services buyers say AI improved their experience, vs. 44% across industries. — [Invoca report](https://www.invoca.com/reports/home-services-buyer-experience-report-2026)
- 36% of homeowners "draw the line" at AI replacing HVAC expertise. — [ACHR News](https://www.achrnews.com/articles/166872-36-of-homeowners-draw-the-line-at-ai-replacing-hvac-expertise)
- Emergency triage (routing true emergencies to on-call techs) and bilingual Spanish intake are standard vendor claims. One vendor asserts, without a source, that contractors lacking Spanish miss 15% to 25% of potential customers in Hispanic-majority markets. — [TurboCall](https://turbocall.net/use-cases/home-services/); [Sameday](https://sameday.ai/post/spanish-speaking-ai-receptionists-a-must-have-for-home-service-contractors)
- Membership and maintenance-agreement handling appears in vendor copy only as renewals and reminders, not as active selling on the call. — [Harmony](https://harmony.ai/use-case/home-services)
- Reddit and contractor-forum sentiment: searches returned no usable HVAC-owner threads. The Jobber community has a 2026 thread on AI use. — [Jobber Community](https://community.getjobber.com/discussions/operations-forum/ai-for-contractors-how-home-service-pros-are-using-ai-in-2026/9398)

### Inferences
- The "customers hate robots" objection can be answered with resolution: ServiceForge's own data says 73% prioritize resolution over speed. An agent that books, quotes and dispatches beats one that only takes messages. A visible warm transfer to a human is a likely trust feature.
- Likely gaps where differentiation is possible, based on absence of evidence and not confirmed by surveys:
  - Selling membership and maintenance agreements on the inbound call. This is the main revenue lever in Nexstar-style and PE playbooks.
  - Financing pre-qualification.
  - Price-range quoting tied to the shop's price book.
  - True Spanish-first flows.
  - Outbound reactivation of unsold estimates and lapsed members. Avoca follows up estimates, but small-shop tools mostly don't.
- Contractors' stated barrier is complexity, not price. Done-for-you setup and FSM-native integration is a wedge.

### Gaps
- No ACCA, PHCC, Nexstar or Service Nation survey data on AI answering was found.
- No Reddit or Facebook-group evidence was retrieved.
- No quantified ranking of "missing features" from contractor surveys.

## 4. Underserved sub-trades and workflows; pricing models

### Takeaway
The FSM incumbents now bundle cheap AI receptionists: Jobber at $29/mo, ServiceTitan AI Voice Agent reportedly $2.75/call, and Housecall Pro CSR AI. This commoditizes basic answering for their user bases. Vendor attention concentrates on HVAC and plumbing (Avoca's core). Restoration, septic, pool, pest, garage door and appliance repair appear underserved, but this is inferred from silence rather than proven. Pricing in the market is mostly per-minute or capped flat-rate. True per-booked-job pricing is rare, which is a possible differentiation angle.

### Cited Findings
- Jobber Receptionist: general availability August 2025. $29/mo add-on with 30 conversations, $0.79 per additional conversation, unlimited on the Plus plan. — [fasite comparison](https://fasite.org/resources/jobber-housecall-pro-servicetitan-ai-receptionist); [botcadence](https://botcadence.com/blogs/jobber-vs-housecall-pro-vs-servicetitan-ai-receptionist)
- Housecall Pro CSR AI: add-on, pricing by demo only. ServiceTitan AI Voice Agent: reportedly $2.75 per call with no minimum (from a Jan 29 2026 podcast page), but other sources say no pricing is published. Unconfirmed. — [levelnex](https://www.levelnex.ai/insights/fsm-ai-receptionist-transparency); [getlocalcall](https://getlocalcall.com/servicetitan-pricing)
- Between August 2025 and April 2026, Jobber, Housecall Pro and ServiceTitan all shipped native AI phone answering. — [withallo / astucia summaries](https://astucia.io/blog/best-ai-receptionist-breezy-shutdown)
- Market pricing: per-minute from about $0.07/min. Flat plans such as $97.50/mo for 30 calls. Premium quote-based platforms estimated at $1,000 to $3,000/mo. True outcome or booked-job billing is rare. One guide says "the cheapest per-minute rate is often the most expensive per booked meeting." — [getnextphone pricing guide 2026](https://www.getnextphone.com/blog/ai-receptionist-pricing); [oncehub 2026](https://www.oncehub.com/blog/answering-service-cost); [eseospace](https://eseospace.com/blog/ai-phone-billing-models/)
- Per-minute pricing has a structural problem: bills peak in the busiest months, which are seasonal HVAC peaks. — [getnextphone](https://www.getnextphone.com/blog/ai-receptionist-pricing)
- Avoca's customers are mainly HVAC and plumbing. It plans to expand into moving, junk removal, automotive and property management, not restoration, septic or pool. — [Pulse 2.0](https://pulse2.com/avoca-125-million-raised-at-1-billion-valuation-to-power-services-economy-with-ai/); [Kleiner Perkins](https://www.kleinerperkins.com/perspectives/avoca-bringing-ai-to-the-backbone-of-the-real-economy/)

### Inferences
- **White-space hypotheses (to validate):**
  - (a) **Water, fire and mold restoration.** Jobs are high-ticket, emergency-driven and 24/7. Workflows are complex: insurance carrier and claim intake, TPA and program referrals. No vendor targeting was found.
  - (b) **Septic, pool, pest and lawn.** These are route-based recurring-service trades. Their call mix leans toward reschedules, recurring-plan sales and billing, which HVAC-tuned agents underserve. They often use non-ServiceTitan FSMs such as FieldRoutes, PestPac, Skimmer and Jobber.
  - (c) **Garage door and appliance repair.** These are high-volume, price-shopper call mixes where phone quoting and parts and model triage matter.
  - (d) **Mid-size PE platforms and franchisors** below the Apex and Wrench tier.
- Outcome-based pricing (per booked job, or a percentage of booked revenue) aligns with contractor ROI thinking and differentiates against per-minute and per-call incumbents. It requires FSM integration to verify bookings.
- Basic after-hours answering for Jobber, Housecall Pro and ServiceTitan users is being commoditized by native add-ons. A new entrant should not compete there on price.

### Gaps
- No direct survey of contractor pricing-model preference was found.
- Trade-specific AI vendor coverage for restoration, septic, pool and pest was not searched in depth (tool-call limit).

## 5. Recent entrant successes and failures

### Takeaway
The clearest success is Avoca, at a $1B valuation in April 2026 and focused on HVAC and plumbing with PE and enterprise customers. Wrench's selection of Lace AI is a second enterprise win. Failures are less documented. Breezy AI shut down in July 2026 with no stated reason, and FSM-native bundling is a plausible squeeze on standalone SMB tools.

### Cited Findings
- Avoca raised more than $125M (Seed through Series B) at a $1B valuation, announced April 27, 2026. The latest round was led by Meritech and General Catalyst, with Kleiner Perkins and Y Combinator also investing. It reports 800+ customers (secondary source citing Fortune), says it is on track to book $1B in jobs in 2026, and partners with ServiceTitan, Nexstar and 1-800-GOT-JUNK. Analysts flag commoditization risk from foundation-model providers by 2027. — [The AI Insider, Apr 29 2026](https://theaiinsider.tech/2026/04/29/avoca-closes-125m-at-1b-valuation-to-power-americas-services-economy-with-ai/); [Pulse 2.0](https://pulse2.com/avoca-125-million-raised-at-1-billion-valuation-to-power-services-economy-with-ai/); [Enterprise DNA](https://enterprisedna.co/resources/news/avoca-voice-ai-trades-unicorn-2026)
- Bob's Repair (Las Vegas HVAC) adopted Avoca (March 2026 press release). — [Barchart](https://www.barchart.com/story/news/676948/bob-s-repair-implements-avoca-ai-to-streamline-customer-communications-and-improve-call-response-times)
- Lace AI won Wrench Group (March 2026). — [Wrench Group](https://www.wrenchgroup.com/articles/wrench-group-announces-partnership-with-lace-ai)
- Breezy AI (Socratic Systems), an AI receptionist used by home service contractors for after-hours and overflow, permanently shut down July 25, 2026. No reason was given. — [astucia](https://astucia.io/blog/best-ai-receptionist-breezy-shutdown)
- Podium's AI agent count reportedly doubled from about 10,000 to more than 20,000 between January and October 2026. — [Origin Notes Substack](https://originnotes.substack.com/p/podium-gtm-playbook)
- A vendor sales guide says the top churn reason is that contractors "forget the AI is working," and recommends monthly value reports. This is opinion, not data. — [myvoiceaiconnect](https://www.myvoiceaiconnect.com/blog/sell-ai-receptionist-to-home-service-contractors)

### Inferences
- Winning pattern: vertical depth (HVAC and plumbing), FSM integration (ServiceTitan), trade-association channels (Nexstar) and enterprise and PE logos. Losing pattern: horizontal, low-price SMB answering without a distribution moat, now undercut by FSM add-ons. This is suggested by Breezy, but its cause is unconfirmed.
- Retention depends on visible proof of value: booked-job attribution reported to the owner.

### Gaps
- No verified data on Avoca revenue or churn, or on Lace's customer count.
- No systematic list of failed or pivoted entrants. Only Breezy was found.
